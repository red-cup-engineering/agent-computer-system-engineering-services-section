## The insight

ECC scores instincts with a float (0.3-0.9) based on observation count. This is naive — it treats all observations as equivalent, ignores context, and has no theory of what convergence means.

We have the theory. Multiple pieces connect:

### Training as closure
An instinct's confidence is not a number — it is proximity to a fixed point under a closure operator. The instinct is "learned" when applying the training operator produces no further change. This is idempotence, not a threshold.

### The bilattice
The extending operation (⊙) from the predication paper fuses epistemic states. An instinct has both a content-value (what it claims) and a context-value (where it applies). Relevance is their composition, not a scalar.

### Formality progression
F0 → F1 → F2 → F3 IS the learning progression for an office. An instinct at F1 is prose ("do X when Y"). An instinct at F3 is algebraic ("if input matches pattern, apply transform"). Training sharpens the instinct toward formality.

### Competency-based progression
PQS-style qualification replaces observation-count promotion. An instinct doesn't promote when "seen in 2+ projects" — it promotes when it has demonstrated competency in the duty. What counts as demonstration is duty-specific.

### Situated learning
Instincts are situated — they belong to a practice, not to an abstract space. Project-scoping (ECC's v2.1 feature) is a crude approximation. Office-scoping is better. Community-of-practice scoping (which offices share a practice?) is best.

### 3. Can we formalize "demonstrated competency"?

**Answered:** Competency = the instinct's fiber reaching F5.3 (settled AND partnered) on the relevant duty predicates.
